Mark-to-market — fund NAV and IFRS NAV

Two carrying bases. The fund NAV carries the notes at full fair value; the IFRS NAV carries them at amortised cost, where issue costs are released to P&L over the life of the notes rather than expensed on day one. Enter a yield to reprice.

From market move to fund NAV

Each block is an impact on fund NAV, built up from zero. Green adds to NAV, orange reduces it, teal is the net position. The market move is read off the clean price; issue costs and accrued coupon are what separate it from the number NAV actually reports.

Fund NAV — fair value

The notes are a liability, so a fall in their value is a gain to NAV.

IFRS NAV — amortised cost

Issue costs and the issue discount are capitalised at inception and released through the effective interest rate.

Unamortised balance over the life

Issue costs and discount still carried on the balance sheet at each coupon date, releasing to nil at maturity.

Sensitivity

At the valuation date above. Modified duration is about 4.4 years, so roughly €0.22m per basis point.

Pull to par. The fair-value gain is a timing effect, not value created — held to maturity the liability converges on 100 and the mark unwinds to zero. The two NAVs converge at the same point: the unamortised balance reaches nil and the carrying amount reaches par. No optionality is valued; the make-whole call and change of control put are immaterial at current levels.
